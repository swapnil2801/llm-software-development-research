# Presentation Outline

## Slide 1 — Title
The Role of Large Language Models in Automating Software Development Processes

## Slide 2 — Background
- LLMs generate and transform natural-language and programming-language text.
- Coding assistants now support developers inside editors and chat interfaces.
- Automation changes tasks, review practices, and required skills.

## Slide 3 — Problem statement
Speed alone does not prove useful software. Generated output can be incorrect, insecure, hard to maintain, or based on invented dependencies.

## Slide 4 — Objectives and research questions
Present the five research questions from the README.

## Slide 5 — Methodology
Structured literature review; search terms; inclusion/exclusion; evidence matrix; thematic synthesis.

## Slide 6 — SDLC map
Requirements → Design → Implementation → Testing → Deployment → Maintenance → Security.

## Slide 7 — Benefits
- Faster drafting and prototyping
- Code explanation and documentation
- Test and review assistance
- Support for learning and routine work

## Slide 8 — Evidence
Discuss the Copilot controlled experiment carefully: one JavaScript HTTP-server task, 95 professional developers, and a reported 55.8% faster completion for the treatment group.

## Slide 9 — Risks
Hallucinations, insecure suggestions, package hallucination/supply-chain risk, privacy, licensing, bias, context limitations, and over-reliance.

## Slide 10 — Responsible workflow
Prompt → inspect → run tests → security/dependency checks → human review → document AI assistance.

## Slide 11 — Conclusion
LLMs are useful force multipliers for bounded tasks, not autonomous replacements for software engineering accountability.
