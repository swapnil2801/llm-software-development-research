# The Role of Large Language Models in Automating Software Development Processes

A college research project examining how large language models (LLMs) assist software teams across the software development life cycle (SDLC), their measurable benefits, limitations, and responsible-use requirements.

## Research type

This project is a structured literature review and evidence synthesis. It does **not** claim that an original human-subject experiment was conducted. Reported findings are attributed to the cited studies.

## Research questions

1. Which SDLC activities can LLMs support or automate?
2. What benefits have controlled studies and surveys reported?
3. What risks and limitations affect dependable adoption?
4. What human oversight and evaluation practices are required?
5. Which research gaps remain open?

## Expanded analysis

The paper now includes a task-risk automation model, a broader productivity measurement framework, workplace and enterprise evidence, SWE-bench discussion, educational implications, and a NIST-aligned governance framework. The evidence matrix has been expanded with benchmark, field-experiment, enterprise-trial, and governance sources.

## Scope and academic integrity

The paper distinguishes primary study results from synthesis and inference. Numerical claims are not presented as universal guarantees. Before submission, add the student's name, institution, course, supervisor, submission date, and any institution-required citation style.

## Key preliminary conclusion

LLMs can reduce effort on selected, well-scoped development tasks—especially code drafting, explanation, documentation, and test generation—but they do not replace engineering judgment. Verification, security review, testing, dependency checks, and accountability remain human responsibilities.
